New-candidate verdict — read this first

Fable-Fusion / ThinkingCap 27B don't deploy

No decisive quality win. Against stock 27B the finetunes are a wash — FF loses SWE-oracle-40 (47.5% vs 57.5%) but marginally edges LCB-hard (47.2% vs 45.3%); TC ties stock on LCB (45.3%). No suite shows a clear finetune gain, and Q8-dense decode is slow (~30 t/s single-stream). Keep FF as a scaffold / pre-processing candidate only; TC's license gate stays operator-waived with no quality reason to advance.

Laguna-S-2.1 · IQ2 no coding role · parked

The FG program already ruled it out. FG-1: Laguna adds zero unique SWE solves beyond A3∪A4 — redundant, not additive. FG-4: at 44.6 t/s it's ~2× slower than the A4 incumbent (94.5) — the "fast small" story holds only against the slow FF 27Bs and inverts against the real alternative. FG-2: its 26.4% LCB is partly a truncation artifact (5 format-spirals + a loop), but no coding-route case survives even remediated. Its only remaining shot is a non-coding niche (FG-5, unproven). Park it — don't deploy for coding.

A4 · 35B-A3B MoE role confirmed

Throughput king across both eras. The v8 bridge re-confirms v7: 124 t/s single-stream RAG, 270 t/s batched at 2k — ~4× the FF 27Bs. Coding is mixed (bottoms SWE-40 at 32.5% yet tops LCB-hard at 54.7%), and reasoning is statistically NULL across A1/A3/A4 — so no real quality gap to hold against it.

MTP speculative decode workload-gate

Not a free win — workload-dependent. MTP lifts batched short-prompt throughput (FF 2k: 135 vs 102 t/s, +33%), but on single-stream deep-RAG it goes net-negative (FF+MTP 19.8 vs FF 26.4 t/s at 32k) — the draft rarely lands on long contexts. Deploy per-workload, never blanket.

Bottom line: nothing new displaces the lineup on merit — finetunes lateral-or-worse on quality, Laguna ruled out for coding (zero unique solves, 2× slower than A4), A4 the throughput arm, MTP a conditional lever. Decision-support, not a gate; no lineup change is authorized here.

What the architect surface says (v7)

rankingA4 ≫ A3 > A1, everywhere batched

At every batched point the order holds. L=2k peaks: 243 / 153 / 103. The small-active MoE wins on both fronts — fewest active params and clean scaling.

correctionThe dip & collapse are A1-specific

The np=2 dip and long-CoT "collapse" were over-generalized from A1. A4 (also MoE) shows no dip (1.43× at np=2) and batches fine at 32k. A1 alone collapses — confounded across IQ2 / MTP-2 / large-active.

mechanismWhy dense batches so well

Dense amortizes weights across the batch — read 27 GB once, apply to all np tokens. MoE tokens scatter to experts, so its edge pays only at high np with reuse — why A4 climbs to np=32 while A3 plateaus at np=8.

scopeThroughput ≠ quality

Reasoning accuracy was a well-powered NULL across A1/A3/A4 (six suites). Throughput is a legitimate tiebreaker favoring A4 then A3 — but the decision also weighs deployment robustness and the 122B's dual-residency.

Router-integration plan (architect trio)
◧ GATED

The MI210 is today a bench instrument, not a serving node — production is CPU-only. This plan activates only when the GPU is registered as a serving backend in orchestrator_stack.py / model_registry — an operator-gated stack change. Until then it's a parameterization staged and ready.

LAYER 1 · launch-time

Static server sizing

Size each model's launch -np to its expected budget distribution. Short-budget roles → high np; deep-reasoning architect → low np. Never launch A1 at np=2.

LAYER 2 · request-time

Budget → concurrency co-decision

Extend the learned router to emit a concurrency ceiling alongside the budget. Don't admit a request into a slot pool that would exceed np_ceiling(budget) for that arm.

LAYER 3 · live

Admission & placement

Track Σ(budget·kv/token) against the VRAM cap and the bandwidth-collapse ceiling. Near the edge, admit / queue / downgrade — or route to CPU — rather than pack another slot into collapse.
